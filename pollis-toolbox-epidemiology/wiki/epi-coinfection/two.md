# Two Strains

## The Two-Strain Interaction Model

In the Two Strains tab, each host has a state for each strain. A host carrying strain 2 catches strain 1 with its usual chance multiplied by Effect of Strain 2 on Strain 1, and the other way round for strain 2. Competition lies below 1, facilitation above 1, and 1 means no interaction. Writing two different values gives an asymmetric pair, and values may be chosen separately from the transmissions of the two strains.

## What Competition Does to Two Strains

Under competition, a strain already common reduces the chance that infected hosts catch the other. Peaks of the second strain fall, and it may fail altogether if the first has a head start. The total burden need not fall in proportion, however, since a host saved from one strain remains available to it later. Next Steps > Compare Interactions sets no interaction, competition and facilitation side by side.

## What Facilitation Does to Two Strains

Under facilitation, carrying one strain makes hosts more open to the other, which raises coinfection above what chance would give. A helped strain can persist at a transmission too low to sustain it alone. Facilitation can therefore be as important in practice as a measure aimed at one strain: treating a helper can reduce the other strain as well, which a study of each pathogen alone would miss.

## Why the Order of Two Strains Can Change the Result

Next Steps > Order of Arrival runs strain 1 first, strain 2 first and both together. With no interaction, the second strain still meets a host population changed by the first when hosts die or become immune. Under asymmetric effects, the order changes who gains, and its effect can exceed that of either strain's transmission rate. Repeated runs are essential here, because the strain's early course is mostly chance.

## Reading Coinfection in Two-Strain Results

Coinfection peaks when the two curves overlap, so that timing is as important as the interaction itself. Under SIR, staggered strains can miss each other. Next Steps > States of Each Strain shows each strain's states side by side. Coinfected counts should be compared with no-interaction runs of the same strains and timing, and not with a mere product of peak values.
