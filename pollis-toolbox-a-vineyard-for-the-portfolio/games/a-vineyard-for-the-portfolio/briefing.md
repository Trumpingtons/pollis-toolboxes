# A Vineyard for the Portfolio

Harrowgate Drinks, a British drinks group listed in London, sells spirits, beer and cider across the world, and wants a winery for its portfolio. It is looking at three, all family owned:

| Winery | Region | Stated Earnings | Stated Growth | Stated Value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Viña Piedra Azul | Colchagua Valley, Chile | 6.0 m £ a year | 7% a year | 128.4 m £ |
| Tenuta Ventaluce | Tuscany, Italy | 4.5 m £ a year | 4% a year | 58.5 m £ |
| Kestrel Flats Estate | Barossa Valley, Australia | 7.5 m £ a year | 5% a year | 112.5 m £ |

All money is in millions of pounds.

## The Game

- One team is Harrowgate's board; each of the other three teams owns a winery.
- **Due Diligence:** Harrowgate decides how closely to look at each winery. Each winery's owners know something about it that Harrowgate does not, and decide whether to disclose it.
- **First Offers** and **Last Offers:** Harrowgate offers cash and an earn-out for one winery. The winery's owners set the least cash, and the least cash and earn-out, they accept. When the offer meets both, the deal is signed. Harrowgate buys one winery at most.
- **Years 1 to 3:** every winery sets its prices, marketing and investment. Harrowgate sets the distribution and the currency hedge of the winery it owns, whose owners stay on to run it.
- Offers, asks and diligence findings are private. Everyone sees each team's status, earnings, market value and the value it has created.
- Teams can write to each other in Negotiations, beside the chat. The facilitator reads those messages too, and nothing makes a team keep what it promised.
- During the years, the facilitator releases news: harvests, exchange rates and trade. Some news reaches only some roles.

## Value Created

The score of every team is the value it has created, in m £, by the end of Year 3. Money is carried forward at Harrowgate's and the owners' cost of capital, 12% a year:

- **The owners of a winery** started with its stated value. Unsold, they have the earnings they received and the winery's market value at the end. Sold, they have the cash paid and the earn-out due.
- **Harrowgate** started with nothing. It has what its winery earned for it, with the savings of owning it, and the winery's market value, less what it paid, the earn-out due and its diligence.

The three wineries' owners are ranked among themselves. Harrowgate is ranked against doing nothing: above 0, its board created value; below 0, it would have done better to buy no winery and spend nothing on diligence.

The market values a winery at a multiple of its yearly earnings, set by its stated growth: (1 + growth) / (12% − growth).
