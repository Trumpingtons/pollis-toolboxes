# Save the Company

Thistledown Home & Garden runs 140 home-and-garden stores across Britain. Sales have been falling, and it has broken the covenants of its loans. It owes:

| Claim | Owed | Paid | Market Price |
|---|---|---|---|
| Senior loans, from a syndicate of banks, secured on the stores and stock | 220 m £ | first | 65p in the pound, 143 m £ |
| Junior bonds, unsecured | 150 m £ | second | 15p in the pound, 22.5 m £ |
| Shares, held by the founding family and its backers | | last | 3 m £ |

All money is in millions of pounds. Thistledown has a few quarters of cash left.

## The Game

- One team is the shareholders, one the bank syndicate, one the bondholders. The game lasts four or six quarters, as the facilitator chose.
- Each quarter the shareholders can propose a plan: new money they put in, a cut to the banks' loans, a cut to the bonds, and a share of the company for the bondholders. They can also close loss-making stores.
- Each quarter the banks set the largest cut they accept, or call in their loan. The bondholders set the largest cut and the least share of the company they accept, or sell their bonds to a buyer only they know of.
- A plan is adopted when it meets the terms of both the banks and the bondholders, and leaves Thistledown at least 20 m £ of cash to keep trading. Then the game ends.
- When the banks call in their loan, when the cash runs out, or when the last quarter passes without a plan, Thistledown goes into administration, and the game ends.
- Everyone sees each plan proposed and who agreed. The terms each lender set stay private.
- Teams can write to each other in Negotiations, beside the chat. The facilitator reads those messages too, and nothing makes a team keep what it promised.

## What Each Claim Is Worth

- **Under a plan:** Thistledown is worth 10 times its earnings for the coming year, plus its cash and the new money. That value pays the banks' loans as cut, then the bonds as cut, and what is left belongs to the shares, split between the shareholders and the bondholders as the plan says.
- **In administration:** the stores and stock are sold, the administrators take their costs, and what is left pays the banks first, then the bondholders. The shareholders get nothing.

Each team's score is the value of its claim at the end less its market price at the start. Each team is ranked on its own: above 0, it did better than its claim was worth when the game began.
