\nmhead{Self-Study: Learn and Practise}
  {Self-Study: Learn and Practise}
  {Turn your slides, notes and PDFs into what helps you study.}

Turn your slides, notes and PDFs into what helps you study: more problems with their solutions, a topic
explained in depth, a summary of a paper, prose and audio from slides, or slides from a long text. For
students, and for teachers preparing their classes.

Installing the toolbox adds the submenu \menupath{Extend \gt Self-Study: Learn and Practise}. It is a toolbox
of a way of working rather than of a field: Julia computes what has to be right, and Pollis AI+, its study companion,
writes from the material you give it.

\nmsection{Practice}
Multiple-choice exercises on three topics, one tab each: \menupath{Descriptive Statistics} (mean, median,
standard deviation, outliers, correlation), \menupath{Hypothesis Tests} (the $t$ statistic, p-values, the
decision, choosing a test, the chi-square statistic of a $2\times 2$ table) and \menupath{Linear Regression}
(slope, intercept, $R^2$, prediction and interpretation). The data are drawn from the \menupath{Seed} with
\package{Distributions}{https://github.com/JuliaStats/Distributions.jl}, and the answers are computed from
them, so they are right whatever the numbers. Each wrong choice is a usual mistake computed from the same
data, so a student who picks it learns which mistake it was. The same seed gives the same exercise again;
another gives other data and another order of the choices.

\nmsection{Write an Exercise}
An exercise is a Julia function that takes a random number generator and returns the question, the choices
with the correct one first, and the explanation.

\medskip
{\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{30mm}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{58mm}X@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Kind} & \textbf{Data} & \textbf{Use for} \\\midrule
Computed from Data & Drawn from the seed; the choices are computed from them & A statistic, a p-value, a prediction \\
Conceptual & None; the text is fixed and only the order of the choices changes & Choosing a method, reading a result \\\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}}

\medskip
Two choices that come out the same make a version ambiguous, and extreme data make it harder than intended:
\menupath{Choices That Coincide} and \menupath{The Correct Answer Across Versions} find both before a student
does.

\nmsection{Pollis AI+ as Study Companion}
The five entries in preparation share one pattern: you give a file you already have, and you get a file to
study from.

\medskip
{\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{42mm}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{46mm}X@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Entry} & \textbf{You give} & \textbf{You get} \\\midrule
Make More Problems & Slides, notes or a PDF with a few problems & New problems at the same level, with full solutions \\
Explain a Topic in Depth & A paragraph, an area of a page, or a topic & Each concept detailed, with the steps and an example \\
Summarise a Paper & A paper in PDF & Question, data, method, results and limits, as a PDF \\
Slides into Prose and Audio & Slides, in PDF or LaTeX & Continuous text, as a PDF, and an audio file \\
Text into Slides & A long text, a notebook or a LaTeX file & A few slides, as a PDF with their LaTeX source \\\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}}

\medskip
What Pollis AI+ writes is a draft to study from, not a source to cite: read a summary or an explanation beside
the original, which the PDF Viewer of Pollis opens side by side.
