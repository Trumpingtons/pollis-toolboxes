## Paying for a Deal

A deal is paid in cash. Your company pays from its cash first, and borrows the rest from the banks.

## Borrowing

Banks lend a company up to six years of its yearly earnings, less the debt it already has, at an interest rate of 6% a year. Interest is paid each quarter from the quarter's earnings. Debt lowers your company's value one euro for one euro, and a buyer of your company takes on your debt.

## What Your Board Can Pay

Your accounts show your cash, your debt, what you can still borrow, and the most you can pay: cash plus borrowing left. A bid that would cost more than that is not made, and only your board is told so.

The earnings of a company your board buys add to your own, and so to what you can borrow afterwards.
