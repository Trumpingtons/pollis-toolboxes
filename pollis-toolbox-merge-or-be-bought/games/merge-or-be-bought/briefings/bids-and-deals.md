## Bids

Your board can bid for each rival still listed, in cash per share. A bid is made only when your company can pay it: your cash and what you can still borrow must cover the bid times the rival's shares. Finance knows that amount.

## Deals

A company is bought when the highest bid made for it is at least its walk-away price. A company takes part in one deal a quarter, as buyer or as target; when several deals are possible, those that pay the largest premiums over the targets' share prices happen first.

## What a Deal Is Worth

The buyer pays the target's shareholders and takes on its business, its cash and its debt. The savings your analysts estimated come in over the four quarters after the deal. A deal adds to your shareholders' value when those savings, valued like earnings, are worth more than the premium you pay over the target's value.

Bids are public: after each quarter everyone sees every bid made, and a bid raises the price of its target, whether or not the deal happens.
