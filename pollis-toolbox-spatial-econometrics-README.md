# Spatial Econometrics Toolbox

Version 0.1.12

**Spatial Econometrics: Regression Models for Regions.** Models for data on regions, municipalities and other areas, where an area's outcome depends on its neighbours: through their outcomes, their characteristics or the shocks they share. For economists, regional scientists and analysts of regional policy.

Installing it adds the **Spatial Econometrics** submenu to the end of the **Extend** menu; its **Edit** button in the Installed section of the Extensions pane moves it, or any of its panels, to another menu. Each panel has key points, a decision table, example code you can send to the Julia REPL or a notebook, reference wikis, and Next Steps that replicate its results against R and published articles.

## Panels

**Spatial Lag and Error Models**: least squares with Moran's I of its residuals and the Lagrange multiplier tests, and the spatial lag (SAR), spatial error (SEM), spatial lag of X (SLX) and spatial Durbin (SDM) models, with their direct and indirect effects, on SpatialEconometrics.jl. Its Replicate steps reproduce Tables 2, 14, 17 and 18 of Bivand and Piras (2015) from the article's replication archive, and set the panel's estimates beside R's spatialreg on the Columbus and Boston data of the spData package.

**Geographically Weighted Regression**: regressions whose coefficients vary over the map, with a fixed or an adaptive bandwidth chosen by AICc, and multiscale GWR (MGWR), with one bandwidth per coefficient, on SpatialEconometrics.jl. Its Next Steps map the local coefficients, t-values and R-squared, adjust the local tests for multiple testing, test whether a coefficient varies over the map, and compare GWR with least squares and with MGWR. Its Replicate steps set the panel's results beside GWR4 and beside Oshan, Li, Kang, Wolf and Fotheringham (2019), on the Georgia counties of libpysal.

**Spatial Discrete Choice**: binary and ordered choices that depend on the neighbours' choices, on SpatialEconometrics.jl: the spatial lag probit, fitted by linearised GMM or by Gibbs sampling, the spatial ordered probit, and a logistic regression with a spatial filter of Moran eigenvectors. Its Next Steps map the fitted probabilities, draw the trace and density of rho, give the hit rate and confusion tables, check the chain, test the residuals with Moran's I, compute the direct, indirect and total effects on the probability, and compare GMM with Gibbs sampling, the spatial probit with an ordinary probit, and a lag probit with a filter. Its Replicate step sets the panel's estimates beside Table 3 of LeSage, Pace, Lam, Campanella and Liu (2011) on the reopening of New Orleans businesses after Hurricane Katrina, with the data of Wilhelm and de Matos's R package spatialprobit.

**Spatial Interaction Models**: flows between places, such as migration, commuting or trade, on what each origin sends, what each destination attracts and the distance between them: Wilson's unconstrained, production-, attraction- and doubly constrained models, fitted as Poisson regressions with origin and destination effects on GLFixedEffectModels.jl, and a negative binomial model on SpatialEconometrics.jl. Its Next Steps map the flows, the totals and the origin and destination effects, check the fit, overdispersion and Moran's I of the residual flows, compare the four models, exponential and power distance decay, Poisson and negative binomial, the standard errors and least squares on logs, read the distance coefficient in plain numbers, and predict the flows after distances shrink. Its example is the migration between the regions of Austria from PollisDatasets.jl, and its Replicate step sets the four models beside Oshan's (2016) primer on PySAL's spint and spint's tests.

**Spatial Panel Data**: areas observed over several periods, on SpatialEconometrics.jl: spatial lag (SAR), spatial error (SEM) and spatial Durbin (SDM) panels with fixed effects of the areas, of the periods or both, removed by Lee and Yu's transformation or by demeaning, and a dynamic spatial panel with the outcome's own and its neighbours' lags, corrected for the bias of the fixed effects. Its Next Steps test the residuals with Moran's I period by period, map the size of the residuals and the area effects, run the panel Lagrange multiplier tests, compute Driscoll–Kraay standard errors, test for a structural break, compute the direct, indirect and total effects, in the short and the long run for the dynamic panel, and compare the fixed-effect specifications, SAR, SEM and SDM, Lee and Yu's transformation with direct QML, and the static with the dynamic panel. Its Replicate step sets the panel's spatial error estimates beside those of Millo and Piras (2012), with R's splm, on Munnell's production function for the US states.

**Spatio-Temporal Forecasting**: forecasts of areas observed over many periods, from their own past and their neighbours', on SpatialEconometrics.jl: the space-time autoregression (STAR) of Pfeifer and Deutsch, with any number of spatial and temporal lags, the spatial VAR of Di Giacinto, which forecasts several variables together in a causal ordering, and a panel autoregression without space as the benchmark. Each is fitted on the periods before those held back, and its forecasts are scored on them by RMSE and MAE, step by step. Its Next Steps map the forecasts and their errors, draw the forecast paths of chosen areas, test the residuals with Moran's I period by period and with Pesaran's CD test, draw the roots of the system against the unit circle, forecast beyond the end of the data, compare the accuracy of the three models with each area's last value horizon by horizon, powers of the weights with order-k neighbours, and a grid of lag orders, trace space-time impulse responses, and test whether the neighbours matter at all.

**Spatial Causal Inference**: the effects of a policy on the areas under it and on the areas around them, on SpatialEconometrics.jl: a difference-in-differences regression with the share of each area's neighbours under the policy, after Delgado and Florax (2015), in two periods or in every period with fixed effects; and synthetic controls built from comparison areas that leave out the neighbours of the areas under the policy, with the penalty of Abadie and L'Hour (2021). Its Next Steps map the policy, the exposure and the weights of the comparison areas, check what the design can identify before any outcome, check the fit before the policy, and compare the regression with and without the share of neighbours under the policy, the standard errors, the pools of comparison areas, a grid of penalties, and Spatial DiD with Synthetic Control. Its Interpret steps take the effect apart into direct and spillover effects, rank a synthetic control by a permutation test, and estimate the spillover onto the neighbours with synthetic controls of their own. Its examples are the minimum wage panel of US counties of Callaway and Sant'Anna (2021) and the cigarette sales of the US states of Abadie, Diamond and Hainmueller (2010), from PollisDatasets.jl, and its Replicate step sets the synthetic California of Proposition 99, with the predictor weights of the nested search, beside Tables 1 and 2 of that article.

**Shift-Share Designs**: the effect of a change that common shocks bring to each area through its shares in them, on SpatialEconometrics.jl: shift-share instrumental variables and the reduced form, estimated across the shocks as Borusyak, Hull and Jaravel (2022) show, with their standard error clustered by groups of shocks, the confidence interval with the null imposed and the first-stage F, on stacked periods, with the China shock of Autor, Dorn and Hanson (2013) across the US commuting zones as the example. Its Next Steps map the exposure, check the concentration of the shocks, their balance and Moran's I of the residuals, set the standard error beside one clustered by region, show which shocks drive the estimate, as Goldsmith-Pinkham, Sorkin and Swift (2020) do, and recompute it shock by shock. Its Replicate step sets the seven columns of Table 4 of Borusyak, Hull and Jaravel beside Julia's.

**Regionalisation and Spatial Regimes**: contiguous regions and regressions whose coefficients differ from one region to another, on SpatialEconometrics.jl: SKATER regions, after Assunção, Neves, Câmara and Freitas (2006), alike inside in chosen variables, and regime regressions with one intercept and set of slopes per regime, by least squares or with a spatial lag shared by the regimes, with the regimes taken from a column or from SKATER and the Chow test of the split. Its Next Steps map the regimes, their coefficients and the residuals, test the residuals with Moran's I, check the size and spread of each regime, test which coefficients differ, compare numbers of regions, the regimes with a single model, least squares with a spatial lag, a column's regimes with SKATER's regions, and the regimes with geographically weighted regression, and give a bootstrap Chow test that allows for regions found from the data. Its Replicate step sets the regime spatial lag model beside PySAL's spreg on the Baltimore house prices of Dubin (1992).

**Spatial Correlation Robust Inference**: least squares on areas, or on areas over several periods, with standard errors robust to correlation between the areas, on SpatialEconometrics.jl: the spatial HAC standard errors of Conley (1999), with a distance cutoff, a kernel and lags in time; cluster-robust standard errors, by districts or any other column; and the standard errors of Driscoll and Kraay (1998) for panels with many periods. Its Next Steps map the residuals, the neighbours within the cutoff and the clusters, test the residuals with Moran's I, show their correlation by distance, check the neighbours, clusters and periods each estimator leans on, and compare the standard errors across cutoffs, kernels, cluster levels and lags, all the estimators side by side, and least squares with a spatial lag model. Its Replicate steps set the standard errors beside Petersen's (2009) test data, R's plm and fixest.

The [Smart Regions Toolbox](../pollis-toolbox-smart-regions/README.md) reads area files, draws them on base maps, and tests a value of a set of areas for spatial autocorrelation with Moran's I and Local Moran (LISA), the usual first step before a spatial regression.

## Requirements

Julia. Each panel lists the Julia packages it uses on its Powered by line and offers to install the missing ones. The panels read area files through DuckDB's spatial extension, which Pollis downloads from DuckDB's repository when the toolbox is installed, and keeps if the toolbox is uninstalled.
