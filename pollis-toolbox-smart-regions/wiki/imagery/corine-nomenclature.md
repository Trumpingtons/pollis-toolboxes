# CORINE Nomenclature

## The Three Levels of the CORINE Nomenclature

CORINE Land Cover sorts land into 44 classes in a hierarchical nomenclature of three levels ([European Environment Agency (2019)](#ref-EEA2019a); [Feranec, Soukup, Hazeu and Jaffrain (2016)](#ref-FSHJ2016a)). The first digit of a class's code is its level 1 group, the first two digits its level 2 group, and the three digits its level 3 class: 112, Discontinuous urban fabric, lies in 11, Urban fabric, which lies in 1, Artificial surfaces. The five groups of level 1 are Artificial surfaces (1), Agricultural areas (2), Forest and semi-natural areas (3), Wetlands (4) and Water bodies (5). The services of the European Environment Agency label each class with its code and its name, such as `112: Discontinuous urban fabric`, and every year of CORINE Land Cover uses the same classes and colours.

## Minimum Mapping Unit and Minimum Width

CORINE Land Cover maps land in polygons no smaller than its minimum mapping unit: 25 hectares in the status layers of each year, and linear elements, such as rivers, roads and railways, at least 100 metres wide ([European Environment Agency (2019)](#ref-EEA2019a)). A park, a field or a group of buildings smaller than that is part of the polygon around it, under that polygon's class, so a class's area in a small box can differ from what the ground shows. The 100 m raster of each year is made from the polygons. CORINE suits comparisons of regions and of years at the scale of a town or more; the High-Resolution Layers measure single characteristics of the land at 10 m.

## The CORINE Change Layers

The change layers of CORINE Land Cover map the changes of class between two years, with a minimum mapping unit of 5 hectares, finer than that of the status layers ([European Environment Agency (2019)](#ref-EEA2019a)). Each change polygon keeps its class in both years, such as `Code_12` and `Code_18` for the changes from 2012 to 2018, so a change reads as one class turning into another. Change layers exist for 1990 to 2000, 2000 to 2006, 2006 to 2012 and 2012 to 2018 ([Feranec, Soukup, Hazeu and Jaffrain (2016)](#ref-FSHJ2016a); [European Environment Agency (2019)](#ref-EEA2019a)). Since a change can be smaller than the minimum mapping unit of the status layers, comparing two status years is no substitute for the change layer of the same years.
