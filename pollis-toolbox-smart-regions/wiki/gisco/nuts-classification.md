# NUTS Classification

## The Regulation

The NUTS classification is established by Regulation (EC) No 1059/2003 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 26 May 2003, as a common classification of territorial units for the collection, compilation and dissemination of regional statistics in the European Union ([European Parliament and Council of the European Union (2003)](#ref-EPCEU2003a)). Eurostat set up NUTS at the beginning of the 1970s and managed it with the member states through informal agreements for around 30 years; work on the regulation started in spring 2000, and it entered into force in July 2003 ([Eurostat (2026)](#ref-Eur2026c)).

## Three Levels

The NUTS classification is hierarchical: it subdivides each member state into territorial units at NUTS level 1, each of which is subdivided into units at NUTS level 2, each in turn subdivided into units at NUTS level 3 ([European Parliament and Council of the European Union (2003)](#ref-EPCEU2003a)). Eurostat describes NUTS 1 as major socio-economic regions, NUTS 2 as basic regions for regional policies and NUTS 3 as small regions for specific diagnoses ([Eurostat (2026)](#ref-Eur2026c)). GISCO also distributes a country level, NUTS 0, for countries where the nation at statistical level does not coincide with the administrative boundaries ([Eurostat (2026)](#ref-Eur2026a)).

## How Regions Are Defined

The regulation defines NUTS regions primarily from the existing administrative units of the member states, and uses population thresholds for each level, with non-administrative units formed by aggregating smaller administrative units where a member state has no administrative unit of suitable size ([European Parliament and Council of the European Union (2003)](#ref-EPCEU2003a)).

## Amendments and Versions

Amendments to the NUTS classification are adopted in the second half of the calendar year, not more frequently than every three years ([European Parliament and Council of the European Union (2003)](#ref-EPCEU2003a)). Stability ensures that data refer to the same regional unit for a certain time, which matters for time series; when a country changes its regional structure, the Commission amends the classification at the end of the period of stability ([Eurostat (2026)](#ref-Eur2026c)). Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/674 amended the annexes of the regulation with the NUTS 2024 classification ([European Commission (2023)](#ref-EC2023a)).

## NUTS 2024 and NUTS 2027

The NUTS 2024 classification is valid from 1 January 2024 and lists 92 regions at NUTS 1, 244 at NUTS 2 and 1,165 at NUTS 3; the NUTS 2027 classification is valid from 1 January 2027 and lists 91 regions at NUTS 1, 242 at NUTS 2 and 1,170 at NUTS 3 ([Eurostat (2026)](#ref-Eur2026c)). Versions Side by Side in the Eurostat GISCO panel lists the codes a NUTS version added and removed, and the regions whose area changed, against the version before.

## Countries Outside the EU

For the EFTA countries, the candidate countries and potential candidates, a NUTS-like classification is defined by bilateral agreement between Eurostat and their statistical agencies, and GISCO's NUTS files include these regions ([Eurostat (2026)](#ref-Eur2026a)).

## Territorial Typologies

Since 2018, the NUTS regulation also integrates the most relevant territorial typologies, through the initiative called Tercet, so that subnational statistics are coherent and comparable across the EU ([Eurostat (2019)](#ref-Eur2019a)). Eurostat's methodological manual on territorial typologies explains them for data suppliers and users of statistics on areas and regions of the EU. GISCO's NUTS files carry three typology columns, URBN_TYPE, MOUNT_TYPE and COAST_TYPE ([Eurostat (2026)](#ref-Eur2026a)).
