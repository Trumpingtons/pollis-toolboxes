# Licence and Attribution

## The Open Database License

OpenStreetMap is open data, licensed under the Open Data Commons Open Database License (ODbL) by the OpenStreetMap Foundation: anyone is free to copy, distribute, transmit and adapt the data, as long as they credit OpenStreetMap and its contributors, and if they alter or build upon the data, they may distribute the result only under the same licence ([OpenStreetMap Foundation (2026)](#ref-OSMF2026b)). OpenStreetMap's documentation is under a different licence, Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 2.0. Geofabrik's downloads, derived from OpenStreetMap, are under the ODbL 1.0 too ([Geofabrik (2026)](#ref-Geo2026a)).

## How to Credit OpenStreetMap

Wherever OpenStreetMap data is used, the OpenStreetMap Foundation requires two things: credit to OpenStreetMap by its attribution notice, and a statement that the data is available under the Open Database License ([OpenStreetMap Foundation (2026)](#ref-OSMF2026b)). The way the notice is shown depends on the use, a browsable map, a printed map or a static image, as the attribution guidelines describe. A link to openstreetmap.org/copyright makes clear that the data is under the ODbL; data distributed as data should name and link the licence directly, and printed works should give the full URL. The OpenStreetMap Data panel prints © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL with the features it reads and the files it saves.

## Derivative Databases and Produced Works

The ODbL distinguishes a Derivative Database, a database based upon the original, including any modification of it or the extraction of a substantial part of its contents into a new database, from a Produced Work, such as an image, text or sounds made from the contents of a database ([Open Data Commons (2026)](#ref-ODC2026a)). A Produced Work used publicly needs a notice that makes its users aware that its content was obtained from the database and is available under the ODbL; the licence gives as an example "Contains information from DATABASE NAME, which is made available here under the Open Database License (ODbL)". The features the OpenStreetMap Data panel saves as GeoPackage or GeoParquet are an extract of OpenStreetMap's database; a map drawn from them is a Produced Work.

## Share-Alike

A Derivative Database used publicly must be only under the ODbL, a later version of it similar in spirit, or a compatible licence, and anyone who publicly uses it, or a Produced Work from it, must also offer the whole Derivative Database, or a file with the alterations made to the original or the method of making them, in a machine-readable form ([Open Data Commons (2026)](#ref-ODC2026a)). Share-alike does not apply to a Produced Work made from the database, nor to a Derivative Database used only internally within an organisation, which is not public.

## Data From Other Sources in OpenStreetMap

OpenStreetMap's contributors are thousands of individuals, and OpenStreetMap includes openly licensed data from national mapping agencies and other sources, which its copyright page lists by country, among them Ordnance Survey data for the United Kingdom and the data of Spain's National Geographic Institute ([OpenStreetMap Foundation (2026)](#ref-OSMF2026b)). Their inclusion does not mean that the original provider endorses OpenStreetMap, gives any warranty or accepts any liability.

## Usage Policies of OpenStreetMap's Services

The OpenStreetMap Foundation says that, although OpenStreetMap is open data, it cannot provide a free map API or map tiles for third parties, and refers to its usage policies for its API, tiles and Nominatim ([OpenStreetMap Foundation (2026)](#ref-OSMF2026b)). The Overpass API and Geofabrik's download server are services of their own, with their own guidelines: the Overpass API's manual asks users of its public instances to stay within about 10,000 requests and about 1 GB of downloads a day ([Olbricht (2026)](#ref-Olb2026b)).
