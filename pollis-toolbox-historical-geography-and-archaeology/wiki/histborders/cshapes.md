# Historical Borders — CShapes 2.0

CShapes 2.0 is a geographic dataset of the borders of states and dependent territories from 1886 through 2019 ([Schvitz, Girardin, Rüegger, Weidmann, Cederman and Gleditsch (2022)](#ref-SGRWC...2022a)).

## What Does CShapes 2.0 Cover?

CShapes 2.0 extends the earlier CShapes dataset in two ways. It moves the start back from 1946 to 1886, the period that followed the Berlin Conference on the partition of Africa, and it maps colonies and other dependencies as well as independent states, which gives nearly complete coverage of the political units of the world over that time ([Schvitz, Girardin, Rüegger, Weidmann, Cederman and Gleditsch (2022)](#ref-SGRWC...2022a)). The CShapes 2.0 tab of the Historical Borders panel suits questions about the world of nation states and empires since the late 19th century. An earlier From moves to 1886, and a later Until to 2019.

## How Does CShapes 2.0 Date a Change of Border?

Each row of CShapes 2.0 is one shape of one political unit, with the day, month and year on which it starts and on which it ends, the name and code of the unit, its area and its capital. A change of territory ends one row and starts another. As the slider of Historical Borders moves by year, the panel shows in a year the shapes in force on 31 December: a shape that ends earlier in the year is last shown in the year before, and a shape that begins and ends within one year is not shown. The downloaded file keeps the exact dates for an analysis by day.

## Why Can a State Keep One Name for a Century in CShapes 2.0?

CShapes 2.0 follows political units by a code and gives each a name that can serve for the whole life of the unit, combining earlier and later names. A kingdom that becomes a republic can thus remain one unit, where Cliopatria records two polities. History of a Place then gives one long period in the CShapes 2.0 tab and several in the Cliopatria tab. Both are answers to different questions: which state of the international system held the place, and which polity or regime did ([Schvitz, Girardin, Rüegger, Weidmann, Cederman and Gleditsch (2022)](#ref-SGRWC...2022a); [Bennett and 12 coauthors (2025)](#ref-BMTCB...2025a)).
