# Historical Geography and Archaeology Toolbox

**Historical Geography and Archaeology: The Past on the Map.** The borders, places and landscapes of the past, read from open data and mapped in Julia. For historians, archaeologists, heritage officers and museums, and for the municipalities and regional authorities whose places have history attached.

Installing it adds the **Historical Geography and Archaeology** submenu to the end of the **Extend** menu; its **Edit** button in the Installed section of the Extensions pane moves it, or any of its panels, to another menu. Each panel has key points, a decision table, example code you can send to the Julia REPL or a notebook, reference wikis and Next Steps.

## Topic Maps

Maps of a work under one of four topics, each a row of the Topic Maps page with its kind (historical, legendary or fictional), years, region, source and licence, and the Julia code that draws it, which the page sends to the Julia REPL. The events and stages come with the passages of their sources, and the places with their Wikidata items.

| Topic | Maps |
|---|---|
| At War | The Greco-Persian Wars, the Peloponnesian War, the Guelphs and Ghibellines, the Hundred Years' War, the Wars of the Roses, the Napoleonic Wars, the American Civil War, with the battlefields of the American Battlefield Protection Program of the National Park Service, and World War I: the battles, sieges and campaigns that Wikidata gives with a place and a date, with a year slider over the realms of Cliopatria |
| Conquests | Alexander the Great and Julius Caesar's Gallic Wars, from Wikidata, and the conquests of Qin Shi Huang, from chapter 6 of Sima Qian's Records of the Grand Historian, each event with its passage in Chinese and an English summary |
| Places | The places Herodotus names in the Histories, tagged by the Perseus Digital Library, with the coordinates of the Getty Thesaurus of Geographic Names; the places of The Tale of Genji, with their passages of the Japanese text; and the places of The Arabian Nights, with their passages of Burton's translation and the Nights that tell them |
| Journeys | Nellie Bly around the world in 1889 and 1890, day by day; Charles Darwin round the world in HMS Beagle, from 1831 to 1836, from his Journal of Researches; the seven voyages of Zheng He, from the inscriptions of Changle and Liujiagang; the journey of Xuanzang from Gaochang to Khotan, from the Records of the Western Regions of the Great Tang; Marco Polo, in Yule's translation; and the wanderings of Odysseus, each place of legend where an ancient author put it, in that author's words |

The menu has Historical Borders, Historical Maps and Historical Populations; then At War, Conquests, Places and Journeys, which open the Topic Maps page on their topic; then the submenu Archaeological Prospection, which holds Relief Visualisation, Candidate Features, Learned Detection in Lidar and Learned Detection in Satellite Images; then Sites and Monuments. One menu item per panel, which opens on its first tab, or at its first step for Learned Detection in Lidar and Learned Detection in Satellite Images, whose panels have numbered steps.

## Requirements

Julia. Each panel lists the Julia packages it uses on its Powered by line and offers to install the missing ones. Historical Borders and Historical Populations read their files through DuckDB's spatial and excel extensions, which Pollis downloads from DuckDB's repository when the toolbox is installed, and keeps if the toolbox is uninstalled. Both need the Internet to download each source once into their Folder, and for the base map under the slider. Historical Maps needs it for every map it draws, which it reads from IGN's Géoplateforme, the Allmaps servers, the libraries' IIIF image servers and Wikimedia Commons; Georeferenced Maps keeps what it finds in its Folder. The World Cities tab of Historical Populations needs a free account of NASA Earthdata Login, whose username and password the key on the panel's Powered by line keeps in Pollis's Secret Storage. Its HYDE 3.5 tab reads the file population.nc (640 MB), which Utrecht University's server gives only to web browsers: the tab prints the link to download it, and the file goes into the panel's Folder. Sites and Monuments needs it to download Pleiades and the Ancient Near East site placemarks once into its Folder and for its maps: Google's maps need a Google Maps key and Esri World Imagery an ArcGIS key, both kept by the keys on its Powered by line, while its free map needs none. The panels of Archaeological Prospection need it for their example terrain model, which they keep in their Folder, and for the imagery beside their maps. The topic maps need it for the base map, and most of them to read Wikidata, the Perseus Digital Library, the Getty Thesaurus of Geographic Names, Cliopatria and the National Park Service once into the folder their code names.

The data belong to their authors, whose terms apply: [Cliopatria](https://github.com/Seshat-Global-History-Databank/cliopatria), [historical-basemaps](https://github.com/aourednik/historical-basemaps), [CShapes 2.0](https://icr.ethz.ch/data/cshapes/), [European urban population, 700 - 2000](https://doi.org/10.17026/dans-xzy-u62q), [Historical Urban Population: 3700 BC - AD 2000](https://doi.org/10.7927/H4ZG6QBX), [HYDE 3.5](https://doi.org/10.24416/UU01-F45D44), and the maps of IGN under the [Licence Ouverte](https://www.etalab.gouv.fr/licence-ouverte-open-licence/), whose map of Cassini must be cited with the source the panel prints. The maps of Allmaps and the famous maps are under the terms of the libraries that hold them, which the panel prints beside each famous map. Each topic map gives its source and licence on its row of the Topic Maps page: the items of [Wikidata](https://www.wikidata.org) are under CC0 1.0, the texts of the [Perseus Digital Library](https://github.com/PerseusDL/canonical-greekLit) under CC BY-SA 4.0, the [Getty Thesaurus of Geographic Names](https://www.getty.edu/research/tools/vocabularies/tgn/) under the Open Data Commons Attribution License, and Shibuya Eiichi's text of The Tale of Genji on [Wikisource](https://ja.wikisource.org) under CC BY-SA 3.0; the other texts are in the public domain, as are the [battlefield boundaries](https://www.nps.gov/orgs/2287/battlefield-boundaries-map.htm) of the National Park Service.
