# Historical Geography and Archaeology Toolbox

**Historical Geography and Archaeology: The Past on the Map.** The borders, places and landscapes of the past, read from open data and mapped in Julia. For historians, archaeologists, heritage officers and museums, and for the municipalities and regional authorities whose places have history attached.

Installing it adds the **Historical Geography and Archaeology** submenu to the end of the **Extend** menu; its **Edit** button in the Installed section of the Extensions pane moves it, or any of its panels, to another menu. Each panel has key points, a decision table, example code you can send to the Julia REPL or a notebook, reference wikis and Next Steps.

## Newspapers, Encyclopedias and Books

The submenus Newspaper Archives, Encyclopedias, Antique Book Sources and Ancient and Medieval Scripts hold the written sources of the past, each a menu item of its own. A source whose page is not yet written is greyed out in the menu.

| Submenu | Sources |
|---|---|
| Newspaper Archives | ZEN, Delpher, Papers Past, Digi, Europeana Newspapers, American Stories |
| Encyclopedias | Naturalis Historia by Pliny the Elder, Etymologiae by Isidore of Seville, Yongle Encyclopedia, Cyclopaedia by Ephraim Chambers, Encyclopédie, Encyclopædia Britannica, Encyclopedia of China |
| Antique Book Sources | EEBO-TCP, ECCO-TCP, Evans-TCP, Deutsches Textarchiv, Project Gutenberg, Wikisource |
| Ancient and Medieval Scripts | Paleography, Papyrology, Epigraphy, each a list of its sources |

Delpher finds articles in the newspapers of Delpher, the digitised Dutch newspapers of the KB, National Library of the Netherlands, from 1618 to 1995, in four steps. Query counts the articles that have the words between two dates, optionally in one newspaper, of one kind (articles, advertisements, family notices, illustrations with captions) or from one region, year by year; Retrieve gets up to a number of them as a table, with their date, newspaper, title, kind, page, place and link; Read opens an article's text in Pollis's browser with the words of the query marked, beside the link to its scan on Delpher; Save writes the texts as text files, with the table of the articles as a CSV file, in the panel's folder.

Papers Past finds articles in the newspapers of Papers Past, the digitised New Zealand newspapers of the National Library of New Zealand, from 1839, through the DigitalNZ API, in the same four steps. Query counts the articles that have the words between two years, optionally in one newspaper, year by year, and opens the same search on Papers Past, which holds some newspapers further than DigitalNZ does (the Lyttelton Times after 1862, for one); Retrieve gets up to a number of them as a table, with their date, newspaper, title, rights and link, and their texts, which DigitalNZ gives in lower case and without punctuation; Read opens an article's text in Pollis's browser with the words of the query marked, beside the link to its page on Papers Past, where it is as printed beside the scan; Save writes the texts as text files, with the table of the articles as a CSV file, in the panel's folder. DigitalNZ needs no key: a free one, kept by the panel's Credentials line, lifts the limit of 10,000 articles a query.

Digi finds pages in the newspapers of Digi, the digitised newspapers of the National Library of Finland, in Finnish, Swedish and other languages, from 1771 to 1925 for everyone, in the same four steps; Digi divides most of its newspapers into pages, not articles, so a hit is a page. Query counts the pages that have the words between two dates, optionally in one newspaper or in the newspapers of one language, year by year, beside all the pages of those newspapers, and opens the same search on Digi; its search finds the inflected forms of Finnish and Swedish words. Retrieve gets up to a number of the pages as a table, with their date, newspaper, issue, page number, place, language, the words found in the forms they have on the page, a passage around them and the link; Read opens a page's text in Pollis's browser with those words marked, beside the link to its scan on Digi; Save writes the texts as text files, with the table of the pages as a CSV file, in the panel's folder.

Europeana Newspapers finds issues in the newspapers that Europeana holds with their full text, about a million issues given by the libraries of Germany, the Netherlands, Austria, Latvia, Estonia, Finland, Serbia, Poland and Luxembourg, in the same four steps; a hit is an issue. Query counts the issues that have the words between two dates, optionally in one newspaper or in the newspapers of one language, year by year, and opens the same search on Europeana; Retrieve gets up to a number of them as a table, with their date, newspaper, language, library and rights, the words found in the forms they have in the issue, a passage around them and the link; Read opens the text of each of an issue's pages in Pollis's browser with those words marked, beside the link to the issue on Europeana, where the scans are; Save writes the texts as text files, an issue each, with the table of the issues as a CSV file, in the panel's folder. Europeana's search needs a key: the code uses the demo key that Europeana gives for trying its API, and a free key of the user's own, kept by the panel's Credentials line, for more requests.

American Stories finds articles in American Stories, the text of the American newspapers of Chronicling America from 1774 to 1963, read article by article with their headlines and bylines by Dell and nine coauthors (2023), in five steps. American Stories has no search service: each of its years is an archive on Hugging Face, from a few MB for the 1770s to 7.6 GB for 1910, and Julia searches the archives downloaded. Download downloads the years between two dates into the panel's folder once, after showing their sizes, and stops if they come to more than the most allowed; Query reads those years through, about 30 MB of archive a second, for the articles that have the words, optionally in one newspaper or state, and counts them year by year; Retrieve gets up to a number of them as a table, with their date, newspaper, state, page, headline and byline, how often the words are in them and the link to the page on Chronicling America; Read opens an article in Pollis's browser with the words of the query marked; Save writes the texts as text files, with the table of the articles as a CSV file, beside the archives.

Papyrology lists the sources of papyrology, a row per source with what it covers, its period, size, access and licence: the texts, metadata, catalogue records and bibliography of papyri.info (the DDbDP, the HGV, the DCLP, APIS, the Arabic Papyrology Database and the Bibliographie Papyrologique); the catalogues of Trismegistos, the Leuven Database of Ancient Books and Mertens-Pack³; the Demotic sentences of the Thesaurus Linguae Aegyptiae; the Oxyrhynchus Papyri; the CT scans of the Herculaneum papyri of the Vesuvius Challenge; and the data sets of Greek handwriting AL-PUB, ICDAR 2023, Hell-Date and the Zenon papyri. Where a source can be downloaded, its row has the Julia code that downloads it once into the panel's folder, with a function of HistoricalGeography.jl, and opens it as a table in the Table Viewer; the other rows open the source's site in Pollis's browser.

Epigraphy lists the sources of epigraphy in the same way: the Latin inscriptions of the Epigraphik-Datenbank Clauss/Slaby, the Epigraphic Database Heidelberg, Latin Inscriptions in Space and Time and the Epigraphic Database Roma; Roman Inscriptions of Britain and the Inscriptions of Roman Tripolitania; I.Sicily; the PHI Greek Inscriptions; the Inscriptions of Israel/Palestine and the U.S. Epigraphy Project; the cuneiform texts of the Cuneiform Digital Library Initiative and the Open Richly Annotated Cuneiform Corpus; the pre-Islamic Arabian inscriptions of DASI; the medieval and early modern inscriptions of Deutsche Inschriften Online; and the runic inscriptions of the Scandinavian Runic-text Database. Where a source can be downloaded, its row has the Julia code that downloads it once into the panel's folder, with a function of HistoricalGeography.jl, and opens it as a table, and, where its records give coordinates, maps the findspots; the other rows open the source's site in Pollis's browser.

Paleography lists the sources of paleography in the same way: the manuscripts of e-codices, the portal of Biblissima, the Greek manuscripts of Pinakes and the English hands of DigiPal; the abbreviations of Cappelli's Lexicon Abbreviaturarum; the catalogue of HTR-United; and the data sets of transcribed and classified handwriting CATMuS Medieval, CLaMM, DIVA-HisDB, BiblIA for medieval Hebrew, Muharaf for Arabic and Kuzushiji-49 for Japanese. Where a source can be downloaded, its row has the Julia code that downloads it once into the panel's folder, with a function of HistoricalGeography.jl, and opens it as a table, and, for e-codices, Cappelli, CATMuS Medieval, CLaMM and Kuzushiji-49, saves an image of a page, an abbreviation, a line or a character; the other rows open the source's site in Pollis's browser.

Naturalis Historia by Pliny the Elder finds chapters of the Naturalis Historia, the encyclopedia of the Roman world in 37 books, in four steps: Query searches the English translation of John Bostock and Henry Riley (1855) or the Latin text of Karl Mayhoff, both from the Perseus Digital Library and downloaded once into the panel's folder, for the chapters of the books chosen that have the words, joined by AND, OR and NOT, with brackets, phrases and endings written as *, and counts them book by book; Retrieve gets up to a number of them as a table, with their book, its subject, the chapter, the Latin chapters it falls in, its heading, how often the words are in it and its page on Perseus; Read opens a chapter in Pollis's browser, in English and in Latin side by side with the words marked and Bostock and Riley's notes below; Save writes the chapters as text files, in both languages, with the table as a CSV file beside them.

Etymologiae by Isidore of Seville finds sections of the Etymologiae, the encyclopedia of the early Middle Ages in 20 books, in four steps: Query searches the Latin text of Wallace Lindsay (1911), as Bill Thayer gives it on LacusCurtius, a page each book downloaded once into the panel's folder, for the sections of the books chosen that have the words, written as in Naturalis Historia, and counts them book by book; Retrieve gets up to a number of them as a table, with their book, its subject, the chapter and its title, the section, how often the words are in it and its place on LacusCurtius; Read opens the section's chapter in Pollis's browser, the section shaded and the words marked; Save writes the sections as text files, with the table as a CSV file beside them.

Yongle Encyclopedia finds pages of the Yongle Encyclopedia (永樂大典), the encyclopedia of Ming China completed in 1408, of whose 22,877 juan about 800 survive, in four steps: Query asks the search of Chinese Wikisource for the pages that have the words, in traditional or simplified characters, joined by AND, OR and NOT, with brackets and phrases, optionally in one juan, and counts them by ranges of a thousand juan; Retrieve gets up to a number of them as a table, with their text as Wikisource transcribes it from the scans, a transcription made by a program and not yet proofread, the double-line notes in brackets, with their juan, page, heading and link; Read opens a page in Pollis's browser with the words marked, and Wikisource's page with the scan beside the text; Save writes the texts as text files, with the table as a CSV file beside them.

Cyclopaedia by Ephraim Chambers finds entries of the Cyclopaedia, or, An Universal Dictionary of Arts and Sciences of 1728, in four steps: Query downloads the Internet Archive's reading of the Getty Research Institute's scans of its two volumes, A to H and I to Z, once into the panel's folder, splits them into about 8,500 entries at their headwords, and searches the entries, or only their headwords, for the words, written as in Naturalis Historia, an s also finding the f the reading puts for the long s, and counts them letter by letter; Retrieve gets up to a number of them as a table, with their volume, headword, the field the entry names, how often the words are in it and its page of the scan; Read opens an entry in Pollis's browser with the words marked, and its page of the scan at the Internet Archive; Save writes the entries as text files, with the table as a CSV file beside them. The reading is rough, and where it mixes the two columns of a page an entry holds some of the text beside it.

Encyclopédie finds articles of the Encyclopédie of Denis Diderot and Jean le Rond d'Alembert (1751 to 1772), about 74,000 articles in 17 volumes of text, in four steps: Query asks the PhiloLogic service of the ARTFL Encyclopédie of the University of Chicago for the articles of each word or phrase, accents apart unless in quotes, optionally of one class of knowledge or one author, and joins them by AND, OR and NOT, with brackets, then counts them volume by volume with their classes and authors; Retrieve gets up to a number of them as a table, with their volume, headword, author and whether the article is signed, their class as the Encyclopédie and ARTFL's classifier give it, how often the words are in them and their page on ARTFL; Read opens an article in Pollis's browser with the words marked, its page on ARTFL and the scan of its first page; Save writes the articles as text files, with the table as a CSV file beside them.

Encyclopædia Britannica opens Britannica Through the Editions, a page of Pollis: a headword looked up in every edition of the Encyclopaedia Britannica from 1771 to 1929, each edition with its key and years, the article's length in characters and per million characters of its edition, its contributor, its page of the scan at the Internet Archive, and its full text, which the page downloads with PollisBritannica.jl the first time a text is shown. A supplement, or the new volumes of the tenth, twelfth and thirteenth editions, without the article say so. The articles are those of the built-in database PollisBritannica.

EEBO-TCP, ECCO-TCP and Evans-TCP find works transcribed by the Text Creation Partnership, all in the public domain since 2020: EEBO-TCP the 60,000 texts of Early English Books Online, printed in England and in English from 1473 to 1700; ECCO-TCP the 2,500 texts of Eighteenth Century Collections Online; Evans-TCP the 5,000 texts of Early American Imprints, Series I: Evans, printed in America from 1640 to 1800. Each finds them in four steps: Query downloads the TCP's catalogue once from its repository on GitHub and searches the titles, authors and subjects for the words, joined by AND, OR and NOT, with brackets, u and v, i and j, and s and the long s being the same letters, between two years of printing, and counts them decade by decade with their subjects and authors; Retrieve downloads up to a number of their texts from the TCP's repositories on GitHub, once each, optionally keeps those whose texts have words of their own, and gives them as a table with their TCP number, date, author, title, subjects, pages, length in words and how often the words are in them; Read opens a text in Pollis's browser with the words marked, and its repository on GitHub; Save writes the texts as text files, with the table as a CSV file beside them. The three share their catalogue and texts.

Deutsches Textarchiv finds works of the Deutsches Textarchiv (DTA) of the Berlin-Brandenburg Academy of Sciences and Humanities, the German texts of 1600 to 1900, and some before and after, in five steps: Download gets the DTA's catalogue and the plain texts of its core corpus, or of all its texts, for the years chosen, as printed or in modern spelling, once into the panel's folder, saying their size first and stopping beyond a limit; Query searches the texts of the years for the words, joined by AND, OR and NOT, with brackets, optionally of one category, and counts them decade by decade with their categories and authors; Retrieve gives up to a number of them as a table, with their year, author, title, category, licence, length in words and how often the words are in them, and their page at the DTA; Read opens a text in Pollis's browser with the words marked, and its page at the DTA; Save writes the texts as text files, with the table as a CSV file beside them.

Project Gutenberg finds books of Project Gutenberg, the 75,000 books in the public domain in the United States that its volunteers have transcribed, in four steps: Query downloads its catalogue once from the mirror of the Project Gutenberg Literary Archive Foundation and searches the titles, authors, subjects and bookshelves for the words, joined by AND, OR and NOT, with brackets, in a language, by authors who lived between two years, the catalogue giving no year of first printing, and counts them by the quarter century in which their first author died, with their bookshelves, subjects and authors; Retrieve downloads up to a number of their plain texts from the mirror, once each, without Project Gutenberg's header and licence, optionally keeps those whose texts have words of their own, and gives them as a table with their number, title, authors, the first author's years, language, subjects, bookshelves, length in words and how often the words are in them; Read opens a text in Pollis's browser with the words marked, and its page at Project Gutenberg; Save writes the texts as text files, with the table as a CSV file beside them.

Wikisource finds texts of Wikisource, the free library whose volunteers transcribe and proofread texts in more than 70 languages, in four steps: Query asks the search of the Wikisource of a language for the pages that have the words, joined by AND, OR and NOT, with brackets, optionally in one of its categories, and counts them by the works they belong to; Retrieve gives up to a number of them as a table, with their work, the year of the work from Wikidata, their length in words, the words around the first place that has the words and their page on Wikisource, optionally only those whose works have a year between two; Read opens a page in Pollis's browser with the words marked, without Wikisource's headers and notes on the licence, and its page on Wikisource, with the scans where it has them; Save writes the texts as text files, with the table as a CSV file beside them.

## Topic Maps

Maps of a work under one of four topics, each a row of its topic's panel (At War, Conquests, Places or Journeys) with its kind (historical, legendary or fictional), years, region, source and licence, and the Julia code that draws it, which the panel sends to the Julia REPL, an editor or a notebook. The events and stages come with the passages of their sources, and the places with their Wikidata items.

| Topic | Maps |
|---|---|

## Requirements

Julia. Each panel lists the Julia packages it uses on its Powered by line and offers to install the missing ones. Historical Borders and Historical Populations read their files through DuckDB's spatial and excel extensions, which Pollis downloads from DuckDB's repository when the toolbox is installed, and keeps if the toolbox is uninstalled. Both need the Internet to download each source once into their Folder, and for the base map under the slider. Historical Maps needs it for every map it draws, which it reads from IGN's Géoplateforme, the Allmaps servers, the libraries' IIIF image servers and Wikimedia Commons; Georeferenced Maps keeps what it finds in its Folder. The World Cities tab of Historical Populations needs a free account of NASA Earthdata Login, whose username and password the panel's Credentials line keeps in Pollis's Secret Storage. Its HYDE 3.5 tab reads the file population.nc (640 MB), which Utrecht University's server gives only to web browsers: the tab prints the link to download it, and the file goes into the panel's Folder. Sites and Monuments needs it to download Pleiades and the Ancient Near East site placemarks once into its Folder and for its maps: Google's maps need a Google Maps key and Esri World Imagery an ArcGIS key, both kept by its Credentials line, while its free map needs none. The panels of Archaeological Prospection need it for their example terrain model, which they keep in their Folder, and for the imagery beside their maps. Papyrology needs it to download each source once into its folder: 180 MB for the data of papyri.info, 424 MB for ICDAR 2023, a few MB for the others; the index of the Vesuvius Challenge is read again each time. Epigraphy needs it in the same way: 268 MB for the Epigraphic Database Roma, 110 MB for Latin Inscriptions in Space and Time, 60 MB for the Epigraphic Database Heidelberg, 242 MB for the catalogue and transliterations of the Cuneiform Digital Library Initiative, 77 MB for the Royal Inscriptions of the Neo-Assyrian Period in the Open Richly Annotated Cuneiform Corpus, a few MB for the others. Paleography needs it in the same way: 5.5 GB for the training set of CLaMM, 1.3 GB for DIVA-HisDB, 546 MB for BiblIA, 77 MB for Kuzushiji-49, 49 MB for Cappelli's abbreviations, the Parquet file of the manuscript chosen in CATMuS Medieval (16 MB for the one the code names) and its manifests for each library of e-codices; the catalogue of HTR-United is read again each day. Delpher needs it for every step: the KB's search service answers the queries, and its resolver gives the text of each article read or saved, a request each. Papers Past needs it for its first two steps, in which DigitalNZ answers the queries and gives the texts, 100 articles a request. Digi needs it for every step: Digi's search answers the queries, a list of its newspapers comes with each query, and each page read or saved is a request. Europeana Newspapers needs it for every step: Europeana's search answers the queries, and its IIIF and full-text services give the text of each page of an issue read or saved, a request each. American Stories needs it once for each year, to download its archive from Hugging Face, and then not again. Naturalis Historia by Pliny the Elder needs it once, to download its two texts (22 MB) from the Perseus Digital Library's repository on GitHub, and Etymologiae by Isidore of Seville once, to download its 20 pages (2 MB) from LacusCurtius; their links open Perseus and LacusCurtius in Pollis's browser. Yongle Encyclopedia needs it for its first two steps, in which Wikisource answers the queries, 500 pages a request, and gives the texts, 50 pages a request. Cyclopaedia by Ephraim Chambers needs it once, to download the text of its two volumes (9 MB) from the Internet Archive, and for the pages of the scans. Encyclopédie needs it for every step but the second: ARTFL answers each word or phrase of a query in a request, and gives the text of each article read or saved in another. EEBO-TCP, ECCO-TCP and Evans-TCP need it to download the TCP's catalogue (29 MB) once and each text retrieved once, about 300 KB a text, from GitHub. Deutsches Textarchiv needs it for its first step, which downloads the catalogue (4 MB) and the texts of the years chosen, from 8 MB to 145 MB a century, once into its folder. Project Gutenberg needs it to download its catalogue (6 MB) once, and each text retrieved once, about 400 KB a text, from the mirror of the Project Gutenberg Literary Archive Foundation. Wikisource needs it for every step: Wikisource's search answers the queries, 500 pages a request, Wikidata gives the years of their works, 50 a request, and each page read or saved is a request, with a pause of a second between requests. The topic maps need it for the base map, and most of them to read Wikidata, the Perseus Digital Library, the Getty Thesaurus of Geographic Names, Cliopatria and the National Park Service once into the folder their code names.
