# Historical Maps — Famous Maps

Famous Maps is the tab of the Historical Maps panel that shows one of eight famous maps of the world, from the Tabula Peutingeriana to the Tasman map, as an image that moves and enlarges on its own, beside today's map of the region the map shows.

The titles, years and libraries are those of each image's page, which The Famous Maps prints with its terms.

## What Is the Tabula Peutingeriana in the Famous Maps Tab?

The Tabula Peutingeriana of the Famous Maps tab is Cod. 324 of the Austrian National Library: a manuscript map in Latin, dated 1200 to 1220, 340 by 6745 mm, originally a roll and now in 11 segments. The library's IIIF manifest gives an image of the front and of the back of each segment ([Österreichische Nationalbibliothek (2026)](#ref-ON2026a)). The Famous Maps tab shows the front of segment IV, the seventh image of the manifest, beside today's map of Italy and the Adriatic.

## What Is the Mercator Map of 1569 in the Famous Maps Tab?

The Mercator map of the Famous Maps tab is Gerhard Mercator's world map printed at Duisburg in 1569: one map on 18 sheets, a copperplate engraving of 135 by 212 cm, at about 1:20,000,000. The copy shown is that of the University Library of Basel, digitised in e-rara, with the sheets joined into one image ([Mercator (1569)](#ref-Mer1569a)).

## Under Which Terms Are the Images of the Famous Maps Published?

The Famous Maps tab names the terms of each image beside it. The image of the Mercator map carries the Public Domain Mark 1.0 of e-rara ([Mercator (1569)](#ref-Mer1569a)). The manifest of the Tabula Peutingeriana asks that reuse name the Austrian National Library ([Österreichische Nationalbibliothek (2026)](#ref-ON2026a)). The images taken from Wikimedia Commons, and the Universalis Cosmographia, carry Commons' public domain tags: PD-Art marks a faithful reproduction of an old, public domain, two-dimensional work, which has no copyright of its own, and PD-scan does the same for scans ([Wikimedia Commons (2026)](#ref-WC2026a)). The Famous Maps tab reads every image from the server of its library or of Wikimedia Commons as it draws it, and Save the Image saves one to the Folder with its terms.

## How Is a Famous Map Shown Beside Today's Map?

The Famous Maps tab of the Historical Maps panel lays the image on the left of the page, where it can be dragged and enlarged up to twice its own pixels, and today's map on the right, fitted to the region the map shows, outlined in red. The two sides move separately, since the image has no coordinates. The images are at most about 4,000 pixels wide, the size the image servers return when asked for it ([Appleby, Crane, Sanderson, Stroop and Warner (2020)](#ref-ACSSW2020b)); Whole Image opens the image alone in Pollis's browser.
