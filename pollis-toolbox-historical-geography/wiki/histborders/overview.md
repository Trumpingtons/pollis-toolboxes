# Historical Borders — Overview

The Historical Borders panel shows the political map of the past: which states existed in a year, where their borders ran, and how both changed. It opens on Europe and works for any area of the world that its sources cover.

## What Can a Map With a Year Slider Show About History?

A map with a year slider sets the borders of successive years one after another on the same ground. States appear, grow, split, unite and vanish as the year moves, and a town can be seen passing from one realm to another while staying where it is. For a municipality or region, such a map places local history within that of the states around it: the periods of its monuments, archives and place names can be read beside the states that held the territory. History of a Place gives the same succession for one point as a list of states and years.

## How Do I Read the Year Slider of Historical Borders?

Moving the slider, or pressing Play, shows the shapes whose years include the year displayed. The line beside the slider gives the number of states in the Box and the last year in which the source changes a border, so that a map standing still is not read as a period without change. Pointing at a state gives its name and the years of the shape shown, and a click holds them open until the map changes. A name keeps its colour from year to year, and two neighbours can receive similar colours. The names and borders of today are taken off the base map, which is drawn with MapLibre GL JS on OpenFreeMap's tiles of OpenStreetMap, leaving modern coastlines, lakes and land ([MapLibre Contributors (2026)](#ref-MLC2026a); [Ero (2026)](#ref-Ero2026a)).

## Where Do the Borders of Historical Borders Come From?

Cliopatria is a database of political entities of the world from 3400 BCE to 2024, each shape with a range of years ([Bennett and 12 coauthors (2025)](#ref-BMTCB...2025a)). Historical Basemaps, from the historical-basemaps project, is a collection of world maps at chosen years ([Ourednik (2026)](#ref-Our2026b)). CShapes 2.0 maps independent states and dependent territories from 1886 to 2019 with the dates of their changes ([Schvitz, Girardin, Rüegger, Weidmann, Cederman and Gleditsch (2022)](#ref-SGRWC...2022a)). The three datasets were compiled separately, with their own definitions of a state, names and levels of detail, so the same year can differ from one tab to another.

## What Is a Border Before Modern States?

Many polities of the past had zones of influence, overlapping rights and personal bonds in place of surveyed lines. A lord could hold land in two kingdoms, a city could answer to a bishop and to an emperor, and two crowns could share one ruler. The author of historical-basemaps notes that territory and national boundary become meaningful in Europe only from the Peace of Westphalia of 1648, and that the areas of civilisations overlap, especially in ancient history ([Ourednik (2026)](#ref-Our2026b)). A polygon of a medieval realm is therefore one reading of the territory it held, drawn as an area so that it can be mapped.
